7.1Sums and linear combinations

Simulating Y = a₁X₁ + a₂X₂ + … + amXm is just: simulate each Xi independently, then combine — no new theory needed, just the expectation/variance rules from linear combinations.

Worked example. Y=10X₁+25X₂+100X₃, X₁∼N(20,100), X₂∼Gamma(3,2), X₃∼Beta(2,2), independent. Simulate n triples, form Yi, then read off Y, s²(Y), and any probability (P(Y>200), etc.) as sample proportions/statistics — exactly the "simulate then summarize" pattern from the MC integrals page §4.7.

7.2Classic distribution relationships

Lognormal from Normal: X=eZ, Z∼N(μ,σ²) ⇒ X∼Lognormal(μ,σ²)

χ² from Normal: Σi=1k Zi² ∼ χ²k, Zi iid N(0,1)

Exponential is Gamma with shape 1 (already the theme of every Poisson/reliability example)

These relationships are exploited constantly to simplify a simulation: instead of deriving F⁻¹ for an awkward density, recognize it's a known transform of something you already know how to draw.

7.3Mixtures — a different idea entirely

A mixture describes a population made of several sub-populations, each observation coming from exactly one of them:

fX(x) = p₁fX₁(x) + p₂fX₂(x) + … + pmfXm(x)

To simulate: first draw which component (a discrete draw with probabilities p₁,…,pm), then draw from that component's distribution.

Example. Adult height in Norway: women X₁∼N(168, 6.0²), men X₂∼N(180, 6.5²). Overall height is a 50/50 mixture of the two normals — not the sum, and not itself normal (it's bimodal-ish if the means are far enough apart).
Sum vs mixture — the recurring confusion. A sum aX+bY combines both variables in every single draw. A mixture uses only one component per draw, chosen at random. They produce very different shapes even from the same ingredients — always check which one a question actually describes before writing simulation code.

7.4Common exam pitfalls

  • For a linear combination, remember the covariance term matters if the Xi's are not independent (see the Multivariate simulation page).
  • For a mixture, the weights pi must sum to 1 — and simulating "the average of the components" is wrong; you must draw the component label first.
  • Recognize when a question is secretly asking for a known transform (Lognormal, χ²) rather than a fresh inverse-transform/AR derivation.
