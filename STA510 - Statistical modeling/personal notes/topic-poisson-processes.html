8.1Homogeneous Poisson process (HPP)

Four defining properties (rate λ, constant): N(0)=0; independent increments; P(1 event in [s,s+Δs])≈λΔs; P(>1 event)≈0. Consequence:

N(t) ∼ Poisson(λt),  E(N(t))=Var(N(t))=λt

Inter-arrival times T₁,T₂,… iid ∼ Exponential(λ)

Time of the k-th event Sk=T₁+…+Tk ∼ Gamma(k,1/λ)

8.2Simulating an HPP — two equivalent ways

  1. Via inter-arrival times: simulate T₁,T₂,…∼Exp(λ), cumulative-sum them for arrival times, stop once past the horizon.
  2. Via count-then-place: simulate n∼Poisson(λt), then place n arrival times as iid Uniform(0,t) draws, sorted.

Method 2 works because, conditional on N(t)=n, the event times are exactly uniformly scattered over [0,t] — a fact reused constantly for simulation and for conditional-probability questions below.

8.3Worked conditional probability — splitting a Poisson count

P(N(0.1)=0 ∣ N(1)=8). Given the total count in [0,1] is 8, how those 8 events split between [0,0.1] and (0.1,1] is Binomial(8, p) with p = the length ratio 0.1/1=0.1 (this is the uniform-placement fact from §8.2):

N(0.1) ∣ N(1)=8 ∼ Binomial(8, 0.1)  ⇒  P(N(0.1)=0∣N(1)=8) = (1−0.1)⁸ = 0.9⁸ ≈ 0.4305
General recipe — any "conditional count in a sub-interval given the total" question reduces to this Binomial-splitting trick: N(s)∣N(t)=k ∼ Binomial(k, s/t) for s<t.

8.4Worked examples — direct distribution questions

h22: T₁ is the first-event time of an HPP(λ); it's Exponential(λ), so E(T₁²)=2/λ² (from Var+E²=1/λ²+1/λ²).

h24: Newborns at λ=6/day. P(N<3) = e−6(1+6+18) ≈ 0.062. Time to the 3rd birth exceeding 8 hours (=1/3 day) uses the Poisson↔Gamma link directly instead of integrating the Gamma density:

P(T₃>1/3) = P(N(1/3)≤2),  N(1/3)∼Poisson(6×1/3=2)
= e−2(1+2+2) = 5e−2 ≈ 0.677
The trick worth remembering: P(Sk>t) = P(N(t)<k) — rewriting a Gamma-tail probability as a Poisson-cdf is almost always faster than integrating the Gamma density directly.

8.5Non-homogeneous Poisson process (NHPP) & thinning

Same axioms, but the rate λ(t) now varies with time: N(s+t)−N(s) ∼ Poisson(∫ss+t λ(u)du).

  1. Find λmax bounding λ(t) on the interval of interest.
  2. Simulate an HPP with rate λmax, giving candidate times s₁,s₂,….
  3. For each si, accept it (keep it as a real event) with probability λ(si)/λmax — i.e. accept if ui ≤ λ(si)/λmax, ui∼Unif(0,1).
  4. The accepted times form the NHPP — this is exactly Accept-Reject, applied to arrival times.

h24 asks for the expected count under λ(t)=−30t²+350t+3950 over a period — just integrate: E(N) = ∫λ(t)dt (a polynomial, trivial to integrate term-by-term). h25 gives a periodic intensity λ(t)=2+8sin²(π(t−6)/12) — read off λmax=10 directly (since sin²≤1) before setting up thinning.

8.6Common exam pitfalls

  • Don't integrate a Gamma density when a Poisson-cdf rewrite (§8.4) is available — it's almost always faster and less error-prone.
  • For thinning, λmax must bound λ(t) over the whole interval being simulated, not just a local piece.
  • Read the interpretation question carefully — a periodic/non-homogeneous λ(t) is usually meant to model a real seasonal or daily pattern; state what drives the peaks/troughs.
